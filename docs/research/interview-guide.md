# Interview Guide

## Interview Principles

- Do not ask participants to describe traumatic events.
- Let participants skip any question.
- Use scenarios when possible.
- Avoid pressuring people to disclose personal experience.
- End with support resources.
- Keep notes anonymized.

## Opening Script

Thank you for speaking with us. We are researching how people look for safe, practical support when they may be in a controlling or unsafe relationship. You do not need to share personal details. You can skip any question or stop at any time.

We are not testing you. We are testing whether this product idea is clear, safe, and useful.

## Participant Type A: Support Worker / Expert

### Background

1. What kind of support work do you do?
2. What kinds of situations do people usually need help with first?
3. What are common reasons people delay seeking help?

### User Motivation

4. When someone first looks online for help, what are they usually trying to do?
5. What are the most urgent questions they have?
6. What are the most common non-emergency needs?
7. How often are users worried about phone monitoring or digital safety?

### Product Reactions

8. If a website offered AI chat, risk check, safety plan, resources, quick exit, and disguise mode, which parts seem useful?
9. Which parts seem risky or misleading?
10. What should the homepage show first?
11. What should never be hidden behind too many clicks?

### Feature Ranking

Ask them to rank:

- Tonight Safety
- Leave Preparation
- Phone Monitoring Check
- Safe Word / Help Message
- Evidence Timeline
- Find Human Help
- AI Chat
- Risk Check
- Help Someone Else

### Closing

12. If we could build only one feature first, what should it be?
13. What wording would you recommend?
14. What safety warning should always be included?

## Participant Type B: Bystander / Helper

### Scenario-Based Questions

1. Imagine a friend tells you their partner checks their phone and controls who they see. What would you search for?
2. What would you be afraid of doing wrong?
3. Would you want a checklist, a script, hotline numbers, or a chat tool?
4. Would you use a safe-word message generator?
5. What would help you support them without increasing risk?

### Product Questions

6. Which homepage option would you click first?
7. What labels are clearest?
8. What would make you trust the product?
9. What would make you leave?

## Participant Type C: Lived Experience, Low-Risk Context Only

Use only if the participant is not currently in immediate danger and has explicitly consented.

### Safe Framing

1. Without sharing details, what kind of online help would have been useful?
2. What would have felt unsafe to click or read?
3. Would a quick exit button help?
4. Would a disguised weather page help?
5. Would you have saved notes on your device?
6. Would you trust AI for emotional support, practical planning, or not at all?

### Feature Feedback

7. Which is most useful:
   - tonight safety checklist
   - leave preparation checklist
   - phone monitoring check
   - safe message generator
   - evidence timeline
   - AI chat
8. What should the product avoid?

## Usability Test Script

Give the participant one scenario:

> You are worried someone might check your phone later. Use this site to find the safest next step.

Observe:

- first click
- hesitation
- labels they misunderstand
- whether quick exit is noticed
- whether they trust the sidebar
- whether they reach a useful action

Ask:

1. What do you think this product does?
2. What would you click first?
3. What felt safe?
4. What felt confusing?
5. What did you expect but not see?
