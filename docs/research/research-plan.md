# Beacon Research Plan

## Objective

Find out what motivates someone to open Beacon and what quick action window should appear first.

The research should answer:

1. Who is the primary user?
2. What situation makes them open the website?
3. What are they afraid will happen if they use it?
4. What do they need in the first 10 seconds?
5. Which current features are useful, weak, or unnecessary?
6. What should the homepage path be?

## Current Product Risk

Beacon currently has these modules:

- AI chat
- resources
- risk check
- safety plan
- quick exit
- disguise mode
- AI test

The risk is that these are feature-led rather than motivation-led. The product may feel like an anti-violence themed AI website instead of a specific safety action tool.

## Research Phases

### Phase 1: Desk Research

Goal: understand the problem space before contacting participants.

Topics:

- help-seeking barriers in domestic and family violence
- technology-facilitated abuse
- phone monitoring and digital safety
- safety planning practices
- trusted Australian support pathways
- readability and accessibility of existing resources

Suggested sources:

- 1800RESPECT: https://www.1800respect.org.au/
- eSafety Commissioner: https://www.esafety.gov.au/
- ANROWS: https://www.anrows.org.au/
- AIHW family, domestic and sexual violence: https://www.aihw.gov.au/family-domestic-and-sexual-violence
- Ask Izzy: https://askizzy.org.au/

Output:

- 10-15 key findings
- list of common user situations
- list of common barriers
- first draft of user journey hypotheses

### Phase 2: Competitor / Service Review

Goal: understand how existing services organize help.

Review:

- 1800RESPECT
- eSafety domestic and family violence resources
- Ask Izzy
- Daisy app
- Sunny app
- Safe Steps
- Full Stop Australia
- Legal Aid and community legal centre websites

Output:

- competitor matrix
- first-screen pattern notes
- missing opportunities for Beacon

### Phase 3: Expert Interviews

Goal: understand real support patterns without immediately recruiting high-risk users.

Participants:

- case workers
- counsellors
- social workers
- legal support workers
- community support workers
- digital safety specialists

Sample size:

- 5-8 interviews

Output:

- ranked user situations
- safety concerns with the product
- wording recommendations
- feature risks

### Phase 4: Bystander / Helper Research

Goal: understand users who come to help someone else.

Participants:

- friends
- classmates
- colleagues
- family members
- student support staff

Sample size:

- 6-10 interviews or survey responses

Output:

- "help someone else" journey
- safe message / safe word needs
- resource-sharing needs

### Phase 5: Low-Risk Usability Testing

Goal: test whether the product is understandable in the first 10 seconds.

Participants:

- general users
- students
- non-expert testers

Method:

- give scenarios, not personal questions
- ask them to find the right path
- test mobile first

Example scenario:

> You think someone may check your phone later. Find what you should do next.

Output:

- first-click success rate
- time to first useful action
- confusing labels
- trust concerns

## Suggested Timeline

### Week 1

- desk research
- competitor review
- draft user journeys

### Week 2

- expert interviews
- bystander interviews
- early feature validation

### Week 3

- usability testing
- synthesize findings
- decide product direction

## Ethics And Safety

Do:

- use trauma-informed language
- allow participants to skip questions
- avoid asking for graphic details
- give support resources after interviews
- avoid collecting unnecessary personal data
- keep notes de-identified

Do not:

- ask people currently in danger to test the app alone
- ask participants to disclose abuse details
- store sensitive data in shared docs without anonymization
- frame AI as a replacement for human support

## Research Success Criteria

By the end, we should know:

- the top 3 reasons users open Beacon
- the first-screen structure
- which features to keep
- which features to hide or remove
- what the product should be called/positioned as
- what the next MVP should include
